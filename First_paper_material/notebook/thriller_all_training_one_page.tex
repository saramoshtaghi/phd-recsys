\begin{minipage}[t]{0.51\textwidth}\vspace{0pt}\small
This page explains the \textbf{all-users training figure for Thriller}. The numerical examples use $n=500$ synthetic users, added to 53,424 real users. Each synthetic user rates all Thriller books 5 and all other books 1. Each point on the horizontal axis is a separately trained model at a different injection count, not a training epoch. The values below average over recorded real and synthetic training ratings within each book group, with equal weight per rating.

\textbf{The prediction formula.}
For user $u$ and book $i$, both models calculate
\[
\hat r_{ui}=\mu+b_u+b_i+\mathbf p_u^{\mathsf T}\mathbf q_i.
\]
Here, $\hat r_{ui}$ is the predicted rating; $\mu$ is the global mean of the augmented training ratings; $b_u$ is the learned user bias; and $b_i$ is the learned book bias. The vectors $\mathbf p_u$ and $\mathbf q_i$ are learned lists of numbers representing the user and book. Their dot product multiplies matching entries and adds the results. During training, the difference $e_{ui}=r_{ui}-\hat r_{ui}$ between the recorded rating $r_{ui}$ and the prediction is used to update the parameters.

\textbf{Putting the measured components together at $n=500$.}
The same addition holds for group means. The numbers in each line follow the order $\mu$, $b_u$, $b_i$, and $\mathbf p_u^{\mathsf T}\mathbf q_i$:
\begin{align*}
\text{SVD, Thriller:}\quad &2.918+0.210+0.457+0.839\approx4.425,\\
\text{NMF, Thriller:}\quad &2.918-0.890+2.216+0.062\approx4.305,\\
\text{SVD, non-Thriller:}\quad &2.918+0.211+0.150-0.648\approx2.631,\\
\text{NMF, non-Thriller:}\quad &2.918-0.861-1.404+1.721\approx2.374.
\end{align*}
The mean recorded ratings are 4.436 for Thriller and 2.640 for non-Thriller. They are not 5 and 1 because this figure includes real ratings as well as synthetic ratings. Panel E compares these recorded means with the mean predictions.

\textbf{Three results.}
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*,itemsep=3pt,topsep=3pt]
\item \textbf{SVD reconstructs the training ratings more accurately.} In panel A at $n=500$, SVD's mean absolute error (MAE) is 0.277 for Thriller and 0.323 for non-Thriller; NMF's is 1.076 and 0.507. MAE averages $|r_{ui}-\hat r_{ui}|$ for individual ratings, rather than taking the difference between two group means.
\item \textbf{The models allocate scores differently.} In SVD, the book-bias and dot-product components both favor Thriller over non-Thriller. In NMF, book biases strongly separate the groups: $2.216$ versus $-1.404$. The dot products act in the opposite direction: $0.062$ versus $1.721$, partially offsetting that separation. Panel D also shows positive mean user biases in SVD and negative means in NMF.
\item \textbf{Similar prediction patterns can come from different component combinations.} Relative to baseline, both models raise the mean Thriller prediction and lower the non-Thriller prediction. SVD uses signed dot products alongside book biases; NMF combines a large book-bias separation with opposing, non-negative dot products. These are training-fit results. As $n$ increases, both fitted parameters and the proportion of synthetic ratings in the reported averages change.
\end{enumerate}
\end{minipage}

\textbf{This is a subset of the all-users analysis.} The models are exactly the same as on the previous page: they were trained on real and synthetic ratings together. Here, we select only the synthetic users' recorded ratings when calculating the plotted means and errors. Real ratings still influence the fitted book biases and book vectors.

These are deliberately constructed fake profiles. Every synthetic user rates every Thriller book \textbf{exactly 5} and every non-Thriller book \textbf{exactly 1}. Therefore, the desired predictions for these particular training pairs are 5 and 1, rather than the mixed real-and-synthetic means on the previous page. There is no point at $n=0$ because there are no synthetic users to measure.

\textbf{The same formula, evaluated on synthetic ratings.}
\[
\hat r_{ui}=\mu+b_u+b_i+\mathbf p_u^{\mathsf T}\mathbf q_i.
\]
At $n=500$, the measured components are added in that order:
\begin{align*}
\text{SVD, Thriller:}\quad &2.918+0.004+0.429+1.648\approx4.999,\\
\text{NMF, Thriller:}\quad &2.918-1.039+1.986+0.000\approx3.864,\\
\text{SVD, non-Thriller:}\quad &2.918+0.004+0.133-2.061\approx0.995,\\
\text{NMF, non-Thriller:}\quad &2.918-1.039-1.121+0.000\approx0.758.
\end{align*}
The NMF dot products shown as 0.000 are small positive values, rounded here. Panel E shows these mean predictions against the fixed recorded ratings of 5 and 1. Panel A averages the individual absolute errors, $|r_{ui}-\hat r_{ui}|$.

\textbf{Three results.}
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*,itemsep=3pt,topsep=3pt]
\item \textbf{SVD fits the synthetic 5-star ratings more closely.} At $n=500$, its mean Thriller prediction is 4.999 with MAE 0.012. NMF predicts 3.864 with MAE 1.136. Across the displayed injection counts, the mean Thriller predictions move toward 5, with SVD approaching it at smaller injection counts.
\item \textbf{SVD also fits the synthetic 1-star ratings more closely at $n=500$.} Its non-Thriller prediction is 0.995 with MAE 0.006; NMF predicts 0.758 with MAE 0.244. NMF's mean is below 1, so a lower prediction here does not mean a better fit.
\item \textbf{The score components explain the different fits.} SVD adds a positive dot product for Thriller and a negative one for non-Thriller. NMF's dot products are almost zero in both groups, so its predictions come mainly from $\mu+b_u+b_i$. Its book biases separate the groups, while its negative synthetic-user bias lowers both. Each synthetic user has one $b_u$ shared across all books; consequently, the two group means in panel D coincide.
\end{enumerate}
\end{minipage}

\begin{minipage}[t]{0.51\textwidth}\vspace{0pt}\small
\textbf{The same trained models, now used for recommendation.} No parameters are updated here. We score books that each of the 53,424 real users has not rated, rank them, and select the Top-15. Panels B--E average scores over all unrated books within each group for each user, then across users. There are no recorded ratings for these pairs, so no training MAE or fixed targets of 5 and 1 are shown.

\textbf{The measured predictions at $n=500$.} Using the same order, $\mu+b_u+b_i+\mathbf p_u^{\mathsf T}\mathbf q_i$:
\begin{align*}
\text{SVD, Thriller:}\quad &2.918+0.390+0.429-0.173\approx3.563,\\
\text{NMF, Thriller:}\quad &2.918-0.703+1.981+0.071\approx4.267,\\
\text{SVD, non-Thriller:}\quad &2.918+0.390+0.133+0.222\approx3.663,\\
\text{NMF, non-Thriller:}\quad &2.918-0.703-1.114+2.706\approx3.807.
\end{align*}
\textbf{The same NMF compensation pattern appears here.} Book biases favor Thriller, while dot products favor non-Thriller and offset part of that advantage. In this prediction view, SVD's dot-product difference also favors non-Thriller, unlike the all-users training averages. The groups of user--book pairs being measured have changed.

\textbf{What determines the ranking?} For the same user, $\mu$ and $b_u$ are identical across candidate books. The effective ranking score is therefore
\[
s_{ui}=b_i+\mathbf p_u^{\mathsf T}\mathbf q_i.
\]
Let $\Delta$ mean the mean for unrated Thriller books minus the mean for unrated non-Thriller books, calculated within each user and then averaged across users. Thus
\[
\Delta\overline{\hat r}=\Delta\overline{b_i}
+\Delta\overline{\mathbf p_u^{\mathsf T}\mathbf q_i}.
\]
At $n=500$, the mean score gaps are
\begin{align*}
\text{NMF:}\quad &\underbrace{3.096}_{\text{book bias}}-\underbrace{2.635}_{\text{dot product}}\approx\underbrace{+0.461}_{\text{final gap}},\\
\text{SVD:}\quad &\underbrace{0.296}_{\text{book bias}}-\underbrace{0.396}_{\text{dot product}}\approx\underbrace{-0.100}_{\text{final gap}}.
\end{align*}
\textbf{Three results.}
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*,itemsep=3pt,topsep=3pt]
\item \textbf{NMF retains a mean score advantage for Thriller.} Its book-bias advantage is large enough that the opposing dot-product contribution does not cancel it. In SVD, the smaller book-bias advantage is outweighed by the opposing dot-product difference.
\item \textbf{Top-15 recommendations show the exposure change directly.} In panel A, NMF's mean Thriller count increases from 1.730 at baseline to 10.839 at $n=500$; SVD's increases from 1.577 to 2.280. These counts come from ranking each user's candidates, not from converting the group-mean score gap into a count.
\item \textbf{Training fit and recommendation exposure are different outcomes.} SVD fits the recorded training ratings more closely, while NMF recommends more Thriller books to real users. The score decomposition shows the large NMF book-bias separation and its partial compensation by vectors alongside this recommendation outcome.
\end{enumerate}
\end{minipage}
